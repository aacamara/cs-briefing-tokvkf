A sponsor-owned observability company that says it reached $1 billion in revenue, carries $2.4 billion of floating-rate debt, appointed a new CEO on 1 October 2026, and has an agentic AI roadmap that is mostly still in preview. Ten things to know
  1. A new CEO, days into the job. Nandini Ramani, who led an observability, search and compliance portfolio at AWS, became CEO on 1 October 2026; Ashan Willy, CEO since December 2023, became an advisor to the board. No reason was given publicly. Most of the team is new since 2024, and there have been 4 Chief Revenue Officers in four years.[6,7,8,+3]
  2. The CCO seat has no public announcement. No 2025 or 2026 release names a Chief Customer Officer, and the leadership page lists none. The 2026 CRO announcement covers sales, segments and channel but not renewals or customer success, so who owns them is not public.[8,10,11,12]
  3. Growth had stalled before the sale. Revenue held near $242M for three quarters, growth fell to 7%, and net revenue retention slid from 120% to 114%. The buyers' price fell from $96.05 to $87 after growth guidance was cut to 10-11%.[6,54,55,+10]
  4. The buyout left real debt, and lenders have grown a little cautious. A $2.4B first-lien term loan at SOFR + 6.75%, due November 2030, held by 28 lending funds: roughly $250M a year of interest (derived). Lenders marked it at 95 to 98% of par in June 2026, down from about par six months earlier. Caution, not distress; retention is now a credit metric.[64,66,68,+6]
  5. About 1,255 customers are most of the business. Accounts over $100K produced about 84% of revenue at the last report, and 92% of revenue is consumption. Usage health in that cohort is, in effect, the P&L.[61,62]
  6. Pricing is the most common complaint. There are now three meters: data ingested, user seats, and compute units for AI features. Pricing and licensing is the top complaint in a sample of 31 PeerSpot reviews (9 mentions).[20,21,23,+5]
  7. Agentic AI: one product now GA, most still in preview. Autopilot became generally available on 6 October 2026, but it recommends rather than acts and needs Pro or Enterprise plus the Advanced Compute add-on. The agent builder, root-cause analysis and MCP server are still in preview. Scorecard: New Relic 13, Datadog 17 of 21.[31,77,79,+4]
  8. The 2026 Gartner placement is unannounced. New Relic was a Gartner Leader for the 13th straight year in 2025. For the July 2026 report it has said nothing, while Datadog, Dynatrace, Grafana, Coralogix and Chronosphere announced Leader. Confirm internally before customers ask.[90,95,99,+9]
  9. The market grows; peers grow faster. Datadog grew 36% in Q2 2026, Dynatrace ARR 17%, Elastic 15%. Palo Alto bought Chronosphere ($2.95B consideration) and Snowflake bought Observe ($596M) in early 2026. OpenTelemetry lowers switching costs.[120,121,122,+4]
  10. A sponsor exit is the long horizon. Typical private-equity holds point to roughly 2027 to 2030 (an industry norm, not a reported plan). No sale, IPO or link between the CEO change and an exit was found. Any buyer will study retention by cohort, which is CCO territory.[6,146,148]

finances, public era

Nine years of audited numbers, and a slowdown at the end

New Relic filed audited numbers until the quarter ended 30 September 2023. The arc is fast growth, a painful pricing transition in FY2021, a recovery, and then a stall in the final three quarters.

Why the flat quarters matter

The last three public quarters printed $242.5M, $242.6M and $242.8M. With 92% of revenue tied to consumption, flat revenue means customers were optimising usage as fast as new usage arrived. That is the pattern the sponsors bought into, and the one a customer team is best placed to change.[54,55,56,+6]

What the debt means for customer work

At SOFR + 6.75% the term loan cost 10.59% all-in at the end of 2025 and 10.41% in June 2026, about $250M a year on $2.4B (derived; principal may have amortised). Against roughly $1 billion of revenue, that makes cash generation, and therefore gross retention, expansion and cost-to-serve, a priority for owners and lenders. Terms are unchanged and the revolver looks undrawn, but the 28 funds that hold the loan marked it down from about par to 95 to 98% of par in the first half of 2026. That is caution, not distress, and a fund's mark is not a traded price.[64,66,68,+6]

What can and cannot be said about ARR

New Relic stopped reporting ARR after March 2021 ($674M), when consumption pricing made it less meaningful, and stopped reporting anything in November 2023. A November 2025 company blog says it "achieved $1 billion in revenue" without a period. Treat current scale as roughly $1 billion of revenue, reported but not audited; third-party estimates are too wide to add anything.[1,49,51,+5]

agentic AI

Loud on announcements, light on general availability

Agentic AI is the centre of New Relic's 2025 and 2026 messaging. The question for customer conversations is what is generally available, supported and priced today.

Where New Relic stands

New Relic has the broadest list of AI announcements among its peers and, until October 2026, the thinnest generally available evidence. Autopilot is now generally available, but it recommends actions rather than taking them, needs Pro or Enterprise plus the Advanced Compute add-on, is capped at 100 requests an hour, and has no published price. The agent builder, root-cause analysis, Smart Alerts and the MCP server remain in preview. Competitors sell generally available investigation agents with named customers. For customer conversations the gap is commercial clarity: what is GA, what it costs, who is eligible.[87,88,89,+4]

This section is analysis

Everything in this section is the research team's recommendation, built from the evidence above. It is not sourced fact and does not draw on any internal New Relic information.

The first 90 days
Days 1 to 30: listen and baseline
  • Get gross and net retention by cohort, segment and region; agree one definition with Finance.
  • Meet the 20 largest customers and the 20 largest at-risk renewals.
  • Map who owns renewals, expansion and consumption forecasting across CS and Sales.
Days 31 to 60: fix the bill
  • Make consumption forecasting and usage alerts a standard customer deliverable.
  • Put a cost review into every QBR for the over-$100K cohort.
  • Agree with Product a plain-language statement of what AI features are GA, eligible and priced.
Days 61 to 90: make retention visible
  • Publish a monthly retention and consumption-health view the owners can read.
  • Launch a save desk for displacement risks, with a value-proof pack (cost per GB, MTTR, tool consolidation).
  • Set CS targets on dollar retention by cohort, not logo counts.
Questions to ask in week one
CEO
  • What does the board expect from customer retention and expansion in the next four quarters?
  • Where does customer success end and sales begin: who owns renewals, net revenue retention and consumption?
  • Which customer commitments from the previous leadership still stand?
CFO
  • What are current gross revenue retention and net revenue retention, by cohort and by segment?
  • How much revenue sits in the over-$100K cohort today, and how concentrated is it?
  • What do the lenders and owners watch each quarter, and which of it does the customer team move?
CRO
  • Who owns renewals today? The 2026 CRO announcement does not mention them.
  • Which renewals in the next two quarters are at risk, and why?
  • How are Technical Success Managers measured today, and on what quota?
  • What share of losses go to Datadog, Grafana or in-house builds, and on what reason?
CPO and CTO
  • What is the general-availability date and price for Autopilot and the MCP server?
  • What is the plan for the reliability incidents of 2026 (synthetics, alert delays)?
  • What is the 2026 Gartner placement, and how should the field talk about it?
  • Which AI features are GA, priced and FedRAMP-eligible, by edition?
